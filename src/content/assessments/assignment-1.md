## The brief

> Given a set of starting hexagrams with marked changing lines, construct
> each one from its lines and derive the hexagram it transforms into. Then,
> for one trigram used in the brief, place it within both the Xiantian and
> Houtian arrangements and state one concrete difference between what each
> arrangement does with it.

This is a hand-worked test of the notation itself, ahead of any interactive
tool. A strong response shows the construction step (line by line, trigram
by trigram), the transformation step (which lines change, and why the result
follows), and the arrangement step (the same trigram located within two
different orderings) as three distinct, checkable pieces of reasoning — not
a memorized or looked-up answer.

## What you submit

Worked construction and transformation for each hexagram in the brief, in
whatever legible format you choose (diagram, notation, or a mix), with the
changing-line derivation shown explicitly rather than only stated as a
result — plus the short Xiantian/Houtian comparison described above.

The weighted criteria above split across the three abilities this assignment
is built to test: getting the notation right, getting the transformation
rule right, and correctly comparing the two arrangements covered in weeks
6–7.
