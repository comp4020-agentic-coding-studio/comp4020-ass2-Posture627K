## The question

"Taijitu" names more than one image. What actually changed between them, and
what did that change do to how the underlying idea was read?

## What this builds on

Week 2 deliberately withheld any taijitu image while introducing Taiji
itself. This week is where that image finally arrives — as two images, not
one.

## What you should be able to do after this week

Zhou Dunyi's *Taijitu shuo* (1017–1073) describes a five-tier diagram of
concentric circles (Wuji → Taiji → yin-yang duality → the Five Phases → the
myriad things). The familiar swirling black-and-white symbol is a later,
separate image, with documented diffusion from the Ming dynasty onward. The
point of studying both is not that the same idea got a new picture: a
five-tier diagram of discrete stages and a continuous swirling division make
different things easy to see and different things easy to miss. A redesigned
visual form reshapes what the sign communicates — the same lesson the
changing-line mechanism (week 8) taught about structure applies here to
form.
