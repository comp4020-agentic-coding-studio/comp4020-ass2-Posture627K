## The question

Given the eight trigrams and their structural relationships, what is one
documented way of arranging them into a full order?

## What this builds on

Week 5 asked which relationships hold between trigrams regardless of
arrangement, and left open whether the Xiantian arrangement would place
line-inverse pairs opposite each other once trigrams actually had positions
in space.

## Answering last week's open question

It does. The Xiantian ("Before Heaven") arrangement sets the eight trigrams
around a circle, running from Kun ☷ (all yin) through to Qian ☰ (all yang)
in the same binary-counting order a computer would use to count from 0 to
7 — and at every step, the trigram directly opposite a given position is
exactly its line-inverse from week 5: Qian opposite Kun, Zhen opposite Xun,
Kan opposite Li, Gen opposite Dui. That is what "symmetrical" concretely
means here: the arrangement's geometry and the trigrams' own line-inverse
structure agree, rather than one being imposed arbitrarily on the other.

This is a documented Song-dynasty construction credited to Shao Yong
(1011–1077) — not a transmission from antiquity. Anne Birdwhistell's study
of Shao Yong establishes this historically. It is separate from the older
legendary attribution of the trigrams themselves to the culture-hero Fuxi,
via a river dragon-horse or turtle legend — keep the two claims apart: who
is said to have invented the trigrams (legend), and who actually built this
particular circular arrangement of them (a documented 11th-century
scholar). The binary-counting structure of this arrangement is also not
incidental to the rest of the course: it is exactly what a European visitor
found notable enough to send to Leibniz two centuries later (week 11).

## What you should be able to do after this week

Describe the Xiantian arrangement's symmetry in the concrete terms above —
inverse pairs placed opposite each other, ordered by binary count — and
keep the Fuxi legend and Shao Yong's documented construction as two
separate claims. Week 7 introduces a second, genuinely different
arrangement over the same eight trigrams, so that the contrast between the
two is direct rather than remembered from a single description.
