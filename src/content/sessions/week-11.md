## The question

A popular story claims this notation predicted binary computing. What is the
documented version of that story, and where does it stop being documented?

## What this builds on

Week 6 already flagged why this diagram reached Leibniz at all: the
Xiantian arrangement's binary-counting order. This week gives the actual
documented timeline behind that one detail, plus the wider popular claim
built on it, which the notation's own textual and visual history (weeks
9–10) has already trained you to be skeptical of.

## The documented sequence, and where the popular version drifts

Leibniz drafted binary arithmetic independently by 1679 (*De Progressione
Dyadica*). Joachim Bouvet, recognizing the Xiantian arrangement's
binary-counting structure from week 6, sent Leibniz Shao Yong's diagram in
a letter dated 4 November 1701; it reached Leibniz on 1 April 1703; Leibniz
published on binary arithmetic within about a week of receiving it. Each of
those dates is independently documented — the sequence is not in dispute.

What is a genuine, still-live scholarly debate is how independent Leibniz's
own binary work really was once he had seen Bouvet's diagram — a question
this course does not settle, because the sources it draws on do not settle
it either. The popular version of the story skips past that open question
entirely and compresses the whole documented exchange into a single claim
("the I Ching predicted binary computing"), as if Leibniz derived binary
arithmetic *from* the diagram rather than having already drafted it and
then received a diagram whose structure he recognized. A documented
exchange plus an open question about influence is a narrower, more
interesting claim than the popular one-liner — and the gap between the two
is exactly the kind of gap this course's sourcing discipline exists to
close.

## What you should be able to do after this week

State the documented Leibniz–Bouvet sequence in order, and name at least
one way the popular story overstates it (skipping the 1679 draft date, or
treating a live debate as settled either way, are the two clearest ones).
Next week applies that same closing-the-gap skill to a claim you choose
yourself, and to a sign system this course never covered.
