## The question

The same eight trigrams, arranged a second way: what changes, and why would
a system need two arrangements at all?

## What this builds on

Week 6 established Xiantian as an arrangement whose own geometry places
line-inverse pairs opposite each other — and kept that structural fact
separate from Shao Yong's numbering and the later binary-count reading of
the same positions. Houtian ("Later Heaven") arranges the same eight objects
by a completely different logic, which is the point of meeting it directly
after Xiantian rather than months apart.

## A different logic over the same objects

Where Xiantian's order comes from the trigrams' own internal structure
(line-inverse pairs opposite each other), Houtian's order comes
from outside them: it grounds each trigram's position in the
direction-season-family correlations the *Shuogua zhuan* already attributed
to it back in week 4. The clearest example: Li ☲ (Fire) sits at the
direction correlated with summer, and Kan ☵ (Water) sits at the direction
correlated with winter — the arrangement follows the correlations, not a
counting rule. Compare that to Xiantian, where Qian and Kun (Heaven and
Earth) anchor the two poles of that inverse-pair structure — pure yang
opposite pure yin — with no seasonal role at all; the later binary-count
reading of those same two positions is a separate story (week 6).

Two arrangements, two different organizing principles, over exactly the
same eight trigrams from week 4 — neither one is more correct than the
other, because they are not competing answers to the same question.
Xiantian answers a structural question (what is the trigram set's internal
symmetry); Houtian answers a correlative one (how do these eight objects map
onto the natural year). Holding both without collapsing them into one
picture is the actual skill this week is building.
