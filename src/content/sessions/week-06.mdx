## The question

Given the eight trigrams and their structural relationships, what is one
documented way of arranging them into a full order?

## What this builds on

Week 5 asked which relationships hold between trigrams regardless of
arrangement, and left open whether the Xiantian arrangement would place
line-inverse pairs opposite each other once trigrams actually had positions
in space.

## Answering last week's open question

It does. The Xiantian ("Before Heaven") arrangement sets the eight trigrams
around a circle so that at every position, the trigram directly opposite it
is exactly its line-inverse from week 5: Qian opposite Kun, Zhen opposite
Xun, Kan opposite Li, Gen opposite Dui. That is what "symmetrical"
concretely means here: the arrangement's geometry and the trigrams' own
line-inverse structure agree, rather than one being imposed arbitrarily on
the other.

Shao Yong's own numbering scheme for this arrangement runs Qian ☰ (1)
through Kun ☷ (8) — "pure yang" counted first, "pure yin" last. Two
centuries later, a European correspondence read the same eight positions a
second way: a letter to Leibniz mapped them onto binary count order, Kun
(0) through Qian (7) — the reverse of Shao Yong's own numbering (week 11).
Keep the two apart: Shao Yong's ordinal numbering is a documented fact
about his own arrangement; reading it as binary count is a later,
retrospective interpretation applied to the same structure, not something
Shao Yong himself did.

This is a documented Song-dynasty construction credited to Shao Yong
(1011–1077) — not a transmission from antiquity. Anne Birdwhistell's study
of Shao Yong establishes this historically. It is separate from the older
legendary attribution of the trigrams themselves to the culture-hero Fuxi,
via a river dragon-horse or turtle legend — keep the two claims apart: who
is said to have invented the trigrams (legend), and who actually built this
particular circular arrangement of them (a documented 11th-century
scholar).

## What you should be able to do after this week

Describe the Xiantian arrangement's symmetry in the concrete terms above —
inverse pairs placed opposite each other — and keep three claims separate:
the Fuxi legend, Shao Yong's own documented numbering (Qian to Kun), and
the later binary-count reading of the same structure (Kun to Qian, the
reverse). Week 7 introduces a second, genuinely different arrangement over
the same eight trigrams, so that the contrast between the two is direct
rather than remembered from a single description.
